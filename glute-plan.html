    Weeks 1–4

    Learn phase rules

    Reps left means how many more clean reps you could have done. It keeps sets challenging without grinding to failure.

    Warm-up, 6–8 min

    1. Easy bike, walk or jumping jacks2–3 min
    2. Glute bridge15
    3. Mini-band side walk10 steps each way
    4. Bodyweight squat10
    5. Leg swings, front and side10 each
    6. Light sets of the first exercise1–2

    Checkpoints

    Measure in the morning before eating. Hips at the widest point of the glutes, waist at the belly button. Take front, side and back photos in the same spot and light.

    WeekHips cmWaist cmHip thrust

    Strength usually jumps within 3–4 weeks. Visible shape change takes longer, typically 8–12 weeks of steady progress, so compare week 1 with week 12.

    Food & recovery

    • Protein: about 1.6–2 g per kg of body weight a day, spread over 3–4 meals.
    • Eat enough: muscle grows best at maintenance calories or a small surplus. A big deficit makes glute growth very slow.
    • Carbs around training help you lift a bit more, which drives progress.
    • Sleep 7–9 hours. Most of the building happens on rest days.
    • Steps: 7,000–10,000 a day on top of the plan.

    Adjust on the day

    • Tired, slept badly or on your period: do the session but drop one set from each exercise.
    • Sore from last time: train anyway with lighter weights. Soreness fades after the first 2–3 weeks.
    • Sharp pain in a knee, hip or back: stop that exercise and use an easier version. Pain lasting more than a few days is worth showing to a physio.
    • Missed a day: continue with the next session. Don't squeeze two into one day.
